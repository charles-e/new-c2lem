Another book that got a mention on the _A Meal of Thorns_ podcast as alternative history.  In this version of 1923, the city of Cahokia is located where Saint Louis would be and the town is populated by white, black and native american. Native Americans are the majority controlling the city government with a parallel tribal monarchy and, Anopa, the language of the indians is widely spoken.  Joe Barrow, a native american orphan and white partner Phin Drummond are detectives who catch the case of a murder staged to look like an Aztec sacrifice which case then spins into an attempt to overthrow the government so that whites can take power in the crisis. The Klan shows up, so does the Catholic church, so does WWI, as Joe and Phin met during the war. The author uses the Anopa language in fragments throughout the book to establish the aboriginal majority verisimilitude.

Anyway, Joe Barrow big, dark handsome and charismatic hero who plays jazz when not working.  He gets the girl and is wanted by other girls.  Its a cheesy murder mystery, with interesting plot twists on the political intrigues of the civil and aboriginal government.  The prose is strong without being too flashy and the author provides additional explanatory text at the end of the book, long story short in this history, the small pox virus was not nearly as virulent as it was in our timeline so the natives manage to retain a much larger degree of power against the encroaching colonists.  Fun book.
