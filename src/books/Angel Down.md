Recently read [The Sixth Nik](/src/books/The%20Sixth%20Nik.md) which I enjoyed; but it didn't win a Pulitzer so curiosity killed the cat.  And in this case I think that might be a very literal reality because this book is the most graphic, hair-raising yet beautiful portrait of life on the front lines during WWI that I have read (and actually remember).  

Cyril Bagger is the brutally cynical main character, an american soldier and well established malingerer who spends most of his time digging graves, thrown into a squad tasked to "deal with" a "screamer" somewhere in no man's land. "Deal with" is understood to mean that the screamer is presumed to be a badly wounded soldier who may need to be mercifully euthanized.  What Bagger finds in the middle of the battle field, seems at first, to be an angel whose skin literally radiates bright mystical light. Bagger, along with his sidekick, mascot, 14-yo, orphan, naif and under-age enlister Arno, take it upon themselves to carry the "angel" who, for reasons never explained can not walk and barely speaks, back to their company's HQ where they believe that the angel can somehow end the war.  This return journey is immensely complicated because, except for Bagger and Arno, everyone in the squad sees the angel as some other woman (wife, girlfriend, mother) they are devoted to and then decide to steal the angel with fatal consequences as other squad members stop the theft.   

The anti-war message may or may not land with every reader, but if you are not too squeamish, the prose is fantastic and I think the Pulitzer isn't an undeserved award (granted I have not read any competitors.)  It's been a long time since I read Catch-22 and I may have to re-read since it seems to be a possibly strong competitor in the best book about war category.
