<blockquote>
The month of November settled into chill mists, like an old sopha sinking down on its springs. Day upon day, the cold winds off the river stirred slow grey tributaries of fog between the houses, through which the crush of traffic loomed, and darkened as it loomed, as if becoming more solid with each approaching step.
</blockquote>

A charming story set in 1746 New Amsterdam (aka New York). It begins when a young man, a stranger, comes in on the latest ship from England and tries to cash a bill (a letter of credit). The man's name is Richard Smith and is on a mission that he is unwilling to explain to anyone, but he is due another payment in a month.  The town is split over whether he is a wealthy, eligible bachelor or a conman running a long game that is yet to resolve. I found the story interesting but it does not fall into any standard plots: Richard has a series of encounters, misadventures with the citizens and incurs consequences.  The story is told by an anonymous narrator who, at times, will editorialize. 

The meat of the story is wrapped in a wonderful bun that consists of descriptions of life in old new york --  how people still spoke dutch, how there was no one currency since each colony had its own currency, how the colonists were loyal englishmen who hated the pope, the french and the [Pretender](https://www.nls.uk/collections/stories/scottish-history/jacobites-timeline/), how slavery absolutely existed in the colony, how the colonists were still trying to coexist with the indians. In addition to describing the mechanics of life the book uses beautiful prose to describe the weather, nature in general and the geography of the city; how broad is the "broad way", how cold is winter in December.
